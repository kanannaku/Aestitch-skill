# Art Direction Lab — move beyond generic web layout

## Decide the scale of change

- **Micro**: typographic adjustments, colors, borders or hover feedback. Appropriate only after the overall direction is accepted.
- **Meso**: one component or visualization changes while the surrounding page stays.
- **Macro**: fundamentally different thesis, narrative, spatial system or interaction model. If the user rejects the site twice or says the overall art direction is wrong, return to macro. Do not simply swap columns or retint cards.

## Directions must differ at the *mechanism* level

For each direction define:

1. A **creative thesis** rooted in project content.
2. A **signature mechanism** (reading, exploring, discovering, manipulating, comparing, revealing, navigating).
3. A **hero structure** and visual focal strategy: typography/scene/media/visual hierarchy.
4. The **interaction choreography**, including inactive, active, loading, reduced-motion and mobile states.
5. A **constraint** (what this direction deliberately refuses to add).
6. A **short experiential prototype** that can be opened and interacted with.

Examples of *different methods*, not recommended aesthetics:

- A spatial research explorer where relationships are the navigation.
- A typographic artifact where scale, pauses and editorial sequence carry the voice.
- A narrative experiment that changes an illustration as the reader follows a research problem.

Do not copy a reference site's distinctive code, imagery, branding or complete structure. Different words and colors over the same grid is not a meaningful new direction.

## User discovery

Show independent prototypes first, **without deciding which is best**. Accept 'neither' and 'cannot explain why'. Record absolute approval separately from a relative preference. One chosen prototype does not establish a lifelong style.

## Validated evidence

`concept add` stores an HTML prototype; `concept render` captures a viewport; `concept compare` flags identical hashes, repeated thesis/mechanisms, and nearly identical landmarks. These checks detect obvious lack of divergence. A successful comparison **does not establish good art direction or user preference**.
